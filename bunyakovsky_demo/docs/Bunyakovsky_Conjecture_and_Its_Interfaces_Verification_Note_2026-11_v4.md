## 3. T1 — r2 citation audit (queries → authoritative source → outcome)

Method as in v2/v3 (publisher/DOI/arXiv/author-page primaries; snippets for discovery only). `web_extract` backend still down; primaries fetched by `curl` + meta-tag/text extraction, PDFs via pymupdf in the workspace venv.

1. **$\mathbb{F}_2[t]$ inadmissible shift — primary confirmation.** Pollack, *A polynomial analogue of the twin prime conjecture* (PAMS 136 (2008), 3775–3784; PDF at pollack-math.net, read): appendix, verbatim: "In the case q = 2, we can suppose that T(T+1) divides M, since otherwise there are no twin prime pairs P, P + M of degree n." His prediction $R_0(n;M,q)$ contains $\prod_{Q \nmid M}(1-2/|Q|)(1-1/|Q|)^{-2}$, which vanishes at $q=2$ iff some linear $Q \nmid M$ — matches the brute force exactly. Effinger–Hicks–Mullen convention (twins over $\mathbb{F}_2$: $|P_2 - P_1| = 4$, i.e. difference exactly $t^2+t$) confirmed via the zbMATH review of their Oaxaca volume chapter (Springer DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-59435-9_8) and a 2013 thesis reproducing their construction.
2. **Qualitative twins for $q \ge 3$ — theorem.** Burrin–Issac, *Infinitely many twin prime polynomials of odd degree*, Amer. Math. Monthly 128 (2021), 929–935 (abstract + intro read): "it holds true in the setting of finite fields: there are infinitely many pairs of monic irreducible polynomials over $\mathbb{F}_q$ that differ by a fixed constant, for each $q \ge 3$. Elementary, constructive proofs were given for different cases by Hall and Pollack." Their Corollary 1 gives Hall's generic-field construction verbatim: odd prime $l \mid (q-1)$, consecutive non-$l$-th-powers $a, a-1$, then $(X^{lm} - a,\; X^{lm} - a + 1)$ twin for all $m$ — via Capelli's binomial criterion (Lang, *Algebra*, Ch. 6 Thm 9.1). C. Hall, *L-functions of twisted Legendre curves*, J. Number Theory 119 (2006), 128–147, DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2005.10.004 — the location of the twin corollary per Pollack's own citation "[4, p. 140]" and Kowalski's "[20, Cor. 14]"; the paper itself is paywalled, so the corollary is cited through these two independent attributions. Bergström (arXiv:1711.05564, intro) states Hall's theorem for $q > 3$; Burrin–Issac credit Pollack (thesis) with the completion at the remaining fields including $q = 3$. Essay wording follows Burrin–Issac ("Pollack completed the remaining fields") rather than overclaiming Hall.
3. **Quantitative fixed-$q$ results — Sawin–Shusterman.** Kowalski, Séminaire Bourbaki 2021–22, exp. 1193 (*Binary additive problems for polynomials over finite fields*, according to Sawin–Shusterman; PDF read): Theorem 1.1 — let $|k| > 10^6 |k_0|^4$ ($k_0$ the prime subfield), $a \in k[T]$ fixed non-zero: (1) twins $\#\{p : \deg p = d,\; p, p+a \text{ irreducible}\} \sim L_a |k|^d/d^2$ with the strictly positive singular series $L_a$; (2) quadratic Bateman–Horn ($|k|$ odd): $\#\{f : \deg f = d,\; f^2 + a \text{ irreducible}\} \sim (Q_a/2)|k|^d/d$. Footnote: these are not the sharpest forms; "new ideas seem to be necessary to handle the case when $k = k_0$" (prime fields excluded); Remark 7.1: BH for degree-3 $F$ needs "significant new ideas" — so full Hypothesis H at fixed $q$ remains open. Sources: Sawin–Shusterman, *On the Chowla and twin primes conjectures over $\mathbb{F}_q[T]$*, Ann. of Math. 196 (2022), 457–506, arXiv:1808.04001 [37, Th. 1.1]; *Möbius cancellation … quadratic Bateman–Horn*, Invent. Math. 229 (2022), 751–927, arXiv:2008.09905 [38, Th. 1.2] (venue data from the Bourbaki bibliography; 2008.09905 abstract read directly: Chowla + BH degree 2 "for some values of q").
4. **$\mathbb{F}_2$ twin infinitude open.** No proof found in searches (EHM Conjecture 1 for $q=2$; Burrin–Issac 2021 confines its theorem to $q \ge 3$; a 2013 thesis still lists the $q=2$-inclusive conjecture as unproved). Essay states open [O] with the admissible-shift formulation.

6. **Green–Tao 2010 unconditional range — verified, essay KEPT.** Annals page abstract (annals.math.princeton.edu/2010/171-3/p08, read verbatim): "These conjectures [GI(s), MN(s)] have long been known to be true for s = 1 (essentially by work of Hardy-Littlewood and Vinogradov), and were established for s = 2 in two papers of the authors. Thus our results in the case of complexity $s \le 2$ are unconditional." (arXiv id for the record: math/0606088.) r2's hedge resolved; no essay change.
7. **Ingram–Silverman — r2 correct, and the essay error was worse than flagged.** arXiv:0707.2505v2 (PDF read in full). Theorem 1 ($K = \mathbb{Q}$): $\varphi \in \mathbb{Q}(z)$, $\deg \varphi = d \ge 2$, $\varphi(0) = 0$ *but not vanishing to order $d$ at 0*; $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}$ with infinite orbit; then the numerators $A_n$ of $\varphi^{\circ n}(\alpha)$ have primitive divisors for all but finitely many $n$. Theorem 7 (general): $\gamma$ a **periodic** point at which $\varphi$ is not of polynomial type; $\alpha$ wandering; numerator ideals of $\varphi^{\circ n}(\alpha) - \gamma$. Remark 2: "we do assume that 0 is periodic, while Rice allows 0 to be preperiodic" — Rice = B. Rice, *Primitive prime divisors in polynomial arithmetic dynamics*, Integers 7 (2007), A26 (IS ref [22]). Remark 8: nondegeneracy necessary ($\varphi = z^d/F$, $F(0) = 1$, gives $A_n = A_1^{d^n}$). Remark 16: preperiodic $\gamma$ "presumably" true, not pursued. So v3's hypothesis "0 is *not* periodic" was **inverted** — the theorem needs a periodic, non-degenerate reference point $\gamma$; the wandering point is $\alpha$. (Note: the published Cambridge abstract says "0 is a preperiodic point", in tension with the paper's own Theorem 7 and Remark 2; the essay states the theorem as in the paper body and cites Rice for the preperiodic polynomial case.) Counterexample $z^2$, $a = 2$ survives but is now excluded by the correct hypothesis ($z^2$ vanishes to order $d$ at $0$). **Bibliographic fix found in the same pass: venue is MPCPS 146 (2009), 289–302, DOI 10.1017/S0305004108001795** (arXiv journal-ref + Cambridge Core), not 147 as in v3.
8. **Bodin–Dèbes–Najib phrasing — the odd wording is the authors' own.** arXiv:1902.08155 abstract (read): "prove the Schinzel hypothesis for a wide class of them: polynomials in at least one variable over the integers, polynomials in several variables over an arbitrary field, etc." Essay keeps the phrasing (now in quotation marks) and adds a gloss from their Theorem 1.1 ($R$ a UFD, fraction field with product formula, imperfect in characteristic $p$; simultaneous irreducibility at $y = M(\underline{x})$ for Zariski-dense $M$, infinitude replaced by a degree condition).
